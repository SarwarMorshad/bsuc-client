# BSUC Immersive Nakshi Kantha Website — Design & Implementation Brief

**Project:** Bangladesh Student Union Chemnitz
**Core idea:** A digital Nakshi Kantha is gradually stitched and revealed as the visitor scrolls.

This is the canonical creative and implementation brief for designers, developers,
illustrators and content contributors.

---

## 1. Project concept

The website presents BSUC as a living Nakshi Kantha. The visitor starts with an almost
empty cream fabric. As they scroll, threads move across the cloth and borders, maps,
people, landmarks, cultural symbols, event memories and community stories are gradually
stitched into the surface. By the end, the visitor sees one complete Nakshi Kantha
representing the journey from Bangladesh to Chemnitz and the community grown there.

The embroidery is not only a visual style — it is the main storytelling system.

## 2. Main experience

One continuous embroidered story, revealed in order:

1. The first stitch
2. Bangladesh
3. The journey to Germany
4. Chemnitz
5. The student community
6. Community activities
7. Events and memories
8. New-student support
9. The completed BSUC Nakshi Kantha

Scrolling down stitches; scrolling up reverses smoothly. Cinematic, but always easy to
understand and navigate.

## 3. Opening scene

A large piece of cream fabric: subtle woven texture, soft folds, small handmade
imperfections, a lightly embroidered border, gentle directional lighting, a few
unfinished thread ends. Most of the fabric stays empty.

- Headline: **Every journey adds a new stitch.**
- Support: *From Bangladesh to Chemnitz, our community is stitched together by culture,
  friendship, and shared experience.*

Must also include: BSUC logo, main navigation, language control, Join button,
"Scroll to stitch our story" instruction, and an option to skip the immersive experience.

## 4. The first stitch

A thread enters from one side of the fabric (red, green, blue or marigold depending on
the motif). A small needle or highlighted thread tip leads the movement. First stitches
may create a circular sun, a flower, a decorative border, a small Bengali motif, or the
beginning of the Bangladesh outline.

This interaction must clearly teach that scrolling creates the embroidery. Movement
should feel careful and handmade, not mechanical.

## 5. Bangladesh chapter

Motifs: accurate outline of Bangladesh, red sun, rivers, paddy fields, village houses,
rickshaw decoration, water lilies, Bengali floral patterns, traditional boats, Bengali
lettering.

- Headline: **Our story begins at home.**
- Colors: Bangladesh green, Bangladesh red, marigold, madder, cream.
- Large shapes reveal first, then smaller decorative details. Emotional and familiar,
  never overcrowded.

## 6. Journey chapter

A stitched route leaves Bangladesh toward Germany: dotted flight path, small embroidered
airplane, thread clouds, passport-stamp motifs, Dhaka and Chemnitz labels, Bengali and
German words, small stars or navigation marks, changing thread colors.

- Headline: **Across borders, toward a new beginning.**
- Framing may move slowly; motion stays calm. Symbolic rather than geographically
  technical. Communicates transition, courage, arrival.

## 7. Chemnitz chapter

Chemnitz appears as an embroidered miniature, not a realistic 3D city: Karl Marx
Monument, TU Chemnitz, Hauptbahnhof, student residences, city centre, tram,
International Office, Studentenwerk, community meeting location.

- Headline: **A new city becomes home.**
- Landmarks stitch one at a time. Important places carry interactive markers that open a
  readable information panel with practical details.
- Combines BSUC colors with blue and silver accents.

## 8. Community chapter

Embroidered figures appear around the city: new students, senior students, families,
alumni, volunteers, committee members, friends from other communities. Each figure is
connected by visible thread; the section grows more detailed and colorful as it fills.

- Headline: **Different journeys. One community.**
- Short member messages appear as stitched labels or fabric notes, e.g.
  *"The community helped me during my first week."*
- Real member photographs may appear inside embroidered fabric frames.

## 9. Activities chapter

Each activity is one embroidered motif:

| Motif | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Bengali mask | Cultural events |
| Plate and cup | Food gatherings |
| Football and cricket bat | Sports |
| Open book | Workshops |
| Graduation cap | Academic and career support |
| Train and mountain | Trips |
| Helping hands | Student support |
| Musical instrument | Performances |
| Community table | Social gatherings |

Motifs stitch one after another. On completion: title appears, short description becomes
visible, motif moves slightly forward, a link or action becomes available. Animations
stay subtle so the embroidered quality remains the focus.

## 10. Events chapter

Upcoming events appear as fabric patches sewn onto the main Kantha, each with title,
date, location, category, short description, photograph or illustration, and a
registration button.

Examples: Pohela Boishakh, Eid Gathering, Freshers' Welcome, International Festival,
Sports Day, Career Workshop, Community Trip, Cultural Evening.

Stitches appear around each patch border as it attaches to the cloth. Past events remain
as permanent community memories — every event adds another piece to the shared story.

## 11. New-student guide chapter

An embroidered path with connected milestones:

1. Before arriving
2. First seven days
3. Anmeldung
4. Health insurance
5. Bank account
6. Residence permit
7. University registration
8. Accommodation
9. Part-time jobs
10. Life in Chemnitz

Icons: airplane (arrival), house (registration), medical cross (insurance), bank card
(banking), passport (residence permit), graduation cap (university), key
(accommodation), briefcase (employment).

Selecting a milestone opens practical information in a clear content panel. The immersive
section introduces the guide; a separate conventional page holds the full information.

## 12. Community memories chapter

Photographs appear as fabric pieces attached to the Kantha: uneven stitched borders,
slight rotation, handmade labels, dates, short captions, event names, and thread
connecting one memory to another.

Subjects: cultural celebrations, sports events, graduation moments, community dinners,
trips, volunteer activities, university events, new-student gatherings.

Only selected photographs appear in the immersive scene; the full gallery stays simple
and easy to browse.

## 13. Final Kantha reveal

The framing pulls back slowly to show the complete Nakshi Kantha as one connected
composition containing Bangladesh, the journey route, Chemnitz, community figures,
cultural symbols, event patches, the student-support path, shared memories, and BSUC
identity.

- Headline: **Stitched with culture. Connected in Chemnitz.**
- Support: *Every member adds a new stitch to our shared story.*
- Actions: Join the community, View upcoming events, Contact BSUC, Join WhatsApp, Follow
  Instagram, Volunteer, Explore the new-student guide.

## 14. Visual identity

Use the BSUC palette consistently.

- **Cream** — main fabric background and primary reading surface.
- **Bangladesh green** — national identity, important sections, active states, major stitched shapes.
- **Bangladesh red** — the sun, emotional highlights, call-to-action moments, selected thread details.
- **Brand blue** — navigation, practical information, university-related elements, links.
- **Fresh green** — community growth, positive actions, supporting accents.
- **Silver** — borders, metallic details, needles, subtle trim.
- **Madder** — traditional thread accents and decorative floral motifs.
- **Marigold** — warm highlights, celebrations, ornamental stitching.
- **Indigo** — depth, evening scenes, city backgrounds, high-contrast sections.

## 15. Color balance

Not every brand color is used equally.

- Foundation: **cream, Bangladesh green, indigo**
- Supporting accents: red, blue, marigold, madder, silver

Red is reserved for important highlights. Marigold is used for warmth and celebration.
Blue is used for practical student information and institutional connections.

## 16. Typography

Supports English, Bangla and selected German text.

- **Display font** — large emotional headlines
- **Sans-serif font** — body text, navigation, buttons, event information, student guides
- **Bangla font** — Bangla headings, labels, quotes, cultural content

Example multilingual presentation: স্বাগতম / Welcome / Willkommen.
Bangla is meaningful content, not decorative styling.

## 17. Navigation

Simple and visible throughout: Home, About, Activities, Events, New Students, Community,
Gallery, Contact. Main action: **Join the Community**.

Navigation may use stitched labels or fabric-tab styling, but must stay readable and
familiar.

## 18. Scroll behavior

One continuous scroll timeline. Each chapter has a clear beginning and end. The visitor
always understands what is being stitched, which chapter they are in, how far they have
progressed, and how to skip to regular content.

Scrolling down reveals thread; scrolling up reverses it. Avoid sudden camera movement,
fast spinning, or aggressive zoom.

## 19. Mobile experience

Preserve the story, reduce complexity: simpler stitched illustrations, fewer decorative
particles, less camera movement, avoid long pinned sections, fewer simultaneous
animations, larger text and buttons, event information as standard cards, shorter scene
transitions.

Mobile is not a smaller desktop — it is a clear, lightweight storytelling experience.

## 20. Accessibility

The immersive animation is optional. Provide: skip-immersive link, reduced-motion mode,
keyboard-accessible controls, clear focus states, readable contrast, alternative
descriptions for important visual scenes, standard HTML text for all essential
information, large mobile touch targets, clear button labels.

Reduced-motion visitors see a simplified version where each chapter fades in without
camera movement or extended scroll locking. **The embroidered canvas must never be the
only way to access important content.**

## 21. Performance expectations

Must load quickly for students on mobile devices and average connections. Prioritize
lightweight illustrations, compressed textures, reusable motifs, limited real-time
shadows, selective 3D, lazy loading, simplified mobile graphics, a static fallback image,
and reduced animation quality on weaker devices.

The first visible screen loads before the full immersive experience is ready; a static
poster version of the Kantha appears during loading.

## 22. Content management

The team must be able to update regular content without touching the immersive
animation: upcoming events, event dates, registration links, student guide information,
committee members, contact details, social links, community stories, gallery images,
partner organizations.

The main Nakshi Kantha story stays stable; event and community content updates regularly.

## 23. Development phases

**Phase 1 — Story and interaction prototype:** blank fabric, one animated thread, one
embroidered motif, scroll-controlled reveal, reverse animation on scroll up, final
composition preview. Purpose: prove the stitching interaction feels natural.

**Phase 2 — Visual identity:** fabric style, thread style, border patterns, typography,
color usage, navigation, buttons, event patch design.

**Phase 3 — Main story:** Bangladesh, journey, Chemnitz, community figures, activity
motifs, student guide, final Kantha reveal.

**Phase 4 — Real website content:** events, new-student information, gallery, contact,
membership links, committee information, legal pages.

**Phase 5 — Testing and optimization:** mobile, tablets, desktop, slow internet, keyboard
navigation, reduced-motion mode, browsers, low-performance devices.

## 24. Minimum viable version

1. Empty fabric introduction
2. Scroll-controlled stitch animation
3. Bangladesh motif
4. Journey to Chemnitz
5. Chemnitz motif
6. Community section
7. Four activity motifs
8. Three upcoming events
9. New-student guide introduction
10. Final complete Kantha
11. Join and contact actions

Do **not** begin with a highly detailed city, advanced cloth simulation, or dozens of
animated figures. The stitch-reveal experience is the most important feature and must be
completed first.

## 25. Success criteria

- Visitors understand the Bangladesh-to-Chemnitz story
- The embroidery visibly develops through scrolling
- The animation reverses smoothly
- The final Kantha feels like one complete artwork
- Usable on mobile
- Important information is easy to find
- Visitors can skip the immersive experience
- Reduced-motion users receive a comfortable alternative
- Events and student information can be updated easily
- The design feels culturally meaningful rather than decorative
- The site communicates warmth, belonging, support and community

## 26. Core design principle

Do not simply place Nakshi Kantha patterns around ordinary web sections. The entire
interface should behave like a Kantha being created:

- Journeys become stitched paths
- Events become fabric patches
- Members become embroidered figures
- Memories become sewn photographs
- Activities become symbolic motifs
- New students follow a stitched guide
- The final community becomes one connected composition

> Every student brings an individual story.
> Through community, those stories are stitched together.
